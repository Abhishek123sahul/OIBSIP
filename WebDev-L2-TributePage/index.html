ABOUT HIM

A Life Dedicated to India

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India. He made important contributions to India's space and defence programmes and became widely respected for his work in science and technology.

Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam came from a modest background. Through education, determination and hard work, he built a remarkable career as a scientist and engineer.

He worked with organisations including ISRO and DRDO and played an important role in India's satellite launch and missile development programmes. His contribution to India's strategic and technological development earned him the popular title "Missile Man of India."

Kalam was also deeply connected with students and young people. He encouraged them to dream, learn and work for the development of the country. His simple personality and dedication made him one of India's most admired public figures.

After completing his studies in aerospace engineering, he began his professional career with India's defence research organisation.

Kalam joined the Indian Space Research Organisation and contributed to India's developing space programme.

He played a key role in the development of India's Satellite Launch Vehicle-III programme.

1997

Kalam received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

2002

Became President of India

He became the 11th President of India and served from 2002 to 2007.

2015

Remembered as an Inspiration

Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture to students.
